I have been trying to implement the previously suggested defenses against VM  rollbacks, and have some additional information about the problem. It  appears that hashing the current time into an OS-generated random number is  not sufficient to eliminate the possibility that random numbers will be  reused. This is because time can also be virtualized, and rolled back along  with memory. For example, according to [1], VMware virtualizes all hardware  and software clocks on a virtual machine, except for the CMOS real time  clock (also known as RTC or time of day or TOD). Unfortunately, the CMOS RTC  is typically not accessible from a program running with normal privileges.  And even if the RTC is accessible, its granularity only one second, which  means random numbers can still be reused if a VM snapshot is cloned and then  both clones are restarted at nearly the same time.

VMware does provide a way to automatically resynchronize the time of the  virtual machine after a rollback, by installing a daemon/service on the  guest operating system. However there doesn't seem to be a reason to suppose  that after rollback, the daemon/service will be scheduled to run immediately  by the guest OS, so resynchronization may not occur for some time.

It seems that fixing this problem at the RNG level is not as easy as I  thought, and requires active cooperation between the RNG and the VM  software. That may not be feasible in many situations, so there appears to  be an urgent need for the design and standardization of crypto schemes that  are tolerant of reuse of random numbers, like the SIV mode of operation [2].
