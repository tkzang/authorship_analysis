Another implementation of VMAC is now available as part of the recently  released Crypto++ 5.5 (http://www.cryptopp.com) It further improves VMAC  performance on 32-bit CPUs with SSE2 instructions, and reaches a peak  performance of 3.7 cycles per byte for VMAC-128 on the Pentium 4, making its  speed almost competitive with UMAC on that CPU (and at a much smaller memory  footprint).

I'd also like to point out that VMAC is very easy to parallelize, and should  be very suitable for future generations of CPUs that may have hundreds or  thousands of ALUs. I think this should be part of future algorithm design  considerations (but fortunately I think I just missed the NIST hash  algorithm requirements comment period).
